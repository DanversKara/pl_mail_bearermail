# Download the apps

BearerMail has an app for Android phones and tablets and one for iPhone and iPad. Both
connect to your own BearerMail server and nothing else — your mail is never routed
through anyone else's service.

## Android

**[⬇ Download BearerMail for Android](https://github.com/DanversKara/pl_mail_android_bearermail/releases/latest/download/BearerMail-Android.apk)**

- The newest version, always — the link follows each release.
- Open the file on your phone and allow the install when Android asks. Step by step, and
  what the app can do: [Android app](android.md).
- It works on every Android phone, with or without Google Play services. If you would
  rather have new mail arrive through Google's push service, take
  [the Google version](https://github.com/DanversKara/pl_mail_android_bearermail/releases/latest/download/BearerMail-Android-GooglePush.apk)
  instead — the same app otherwise.
- Every version, with its release notes:
  [all Android releases](https://github.com/DanversKara/pl_mail_android_bearermail/releases).

Once installed, the app updates itself: it checks for a newer release and offers it.

## iPhone and iPad

**[⬇ BearerMail for iPhone and iPad](https://github.com/DanversKara/pl_mail_ios/releases/latest)**

Apple only lets apps onto an iPhone through the App Store or an app signed for that
phone, so installing takes a few more steps than on Android. Everything you need is on
the [iPhone and iPad](ios.md) page.

In a hurry? Open your BearerMail address in Safari, tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**,
and the web app opens full screen like an app, with notifications on iOS 16.4 and newer.

## Which do I need?

| You have | Get |
|---|---|
| An Android phone or tablet | BearerMail for Android |
| An Android phone, and you want Google's push service | BearerMail for Android, the Google version |
| An iPhone or iPad | BearerMail for iPhone and iPad, or the web app on the Home Screen |
| A computer | Nothing to install — use your BearerMail address in any browser |

## Things that bite

- **"Install blocked" or "unknown apps" on Android.** Android asks once per app that
  opens an APK — your browser or your file manager. Allow it for that app, then tap the
  file again.
- **The app opens but cannot connect.** The app talks to your server's address, so the
  server has to be reachable from the phone — on mobile data as well as at home if you
  want mail away from home.
- **Two copies of the app.** The standard and Google versions install side by side as
  separate apps. Keep the one you use and uninstall the other.
